Question

Does the XRP price change the insurance price?

Not the percentages. Insurance (a put option) is priced as a share of the XRP value. Three things set it:

  • Distance: how far the insurance level sits below the XRP price (here ).
  • Time: one month or twelve.
  • Expected movement: the volatility the market prices into options today, read from Deribit. Past moves matter only through it.

So the example uses a round $2.00: at any XRP price the percentages stay the same, only the dollar amounts move with the price. One caveat: if XRP really were at $2.00, the market's expected movement could be different. The example uses today's option prices as if XRP were there.

Method

How the numbers are made

Show the method and the assumptions
  • Market data pulled on : XRP option prices from Deribit (longest expiry ). All dollar figures use XRP at $2.00.
  • Insurance level = the price where the loan would reach 75% of the XRP value. The insurance covers all 10,000 XRP.
  • Crash Shield: real one-month Deribit put prices +25% for the spread; the yearly cost is the average of 20,000 simulated years (XRP volatility 65%), renewed only while the loan is at or below 55%.
  • Hard Floor, Collar, Zero monthly: one 12-month contract with an option dealer. No exchange lists 12-month XRP options, so the price is our estimate: today's Deribit volatility curve stretched to 12 months (12-month at-the-money volatility ), +25% when we buy, 80% of value when we sell. The gains we sell above a cap are valued at the at-the-money volatility only: short Deribit options price big rallies higher, and a dealer will not pay that for 12 months. A dealer quote fixes the real price.

  • Zero monthly: the money from selling the gains above the cap pays the insurance and the 12 months of interest on day 1.
