Three steps, one example

One example runs through the whole page: 10,000 XRP at $2.00. The loan is always 40% of the XRP value.

You pledge XRP

10,000 XRP = $20,000

Your XRP sits in a wallet in your name. We never lend it out or sell it to pay for anything.

2

You get USDC

$8,000 USDC

40% of your XRP value, in USDC (a dollar stablecoin). Use it, repay it whenever you want.

3

We insure your XRP

If XRP crashes, we pay

Every month we buy a "put option": insurance that pays dollars if XRP falls below a set price. The payout reduces your loan. Your XRP is not sold.

Straight answers

The questions everyone asks

Is Crash Shield full protection?

It is full protection against sudden crashes: minutes, hours or days. While Shield is on, the insurance counts as part of your collateral, so your loan can never reach the level where we would sell. It is not a guarantee against a slow fall over many months. Then we ask you to top up at $1.45; if you do, you stay protected. If you want no selling at all, whatever happens, that is Hard Floor.

With Hard Floor, what exactly is guaranteed? Can I end up with more XRP?

For 12 months nothing is sold. You keep exactly your 10,000 XRP. Your value still falls as XRP goes from $2.00 to $1.07, like for any holder, but below $1.07 the insurance pays every cent of the fall, so XRP value minus loan never drops below $2,667.

More XRP? Only if you choose it: instead of repaying the loan, the payout can buy XRP at the low price. At $0.60 the $4,667 buys 7,778 XRP, so you would own 17,778 XRP, but you would still owe $8,000 and need new protection. More upside, more risk. By default the payout repays the loan.

Why does Hard Floor cost so much more than Crash Shield?

Crash Shield insures one month at a time and only while your loan is healthy; when XRP drifts down, you top up instead. Hard Floor insures every possible path for a full year, including long bear markets. XRP has fallen 50% or more at some point in of all 12-month periods since 2024, and the options market prices that in. Today two months of floor cost about 0.84% of the loan while XRP is at $2.00, but after every fall the next two months cost much more. Averaged over 20,000 simulated years with XRP's real volatility, that is 14.2% of the loan a year.

Where does the insurance come from?

From the options market. We buy listed XRP put options on Deribit, the largest crypto options exchange, settled in USDC. Our hedging desk buys them for the whole loan book at once. You never trade options yourself.

Are these numbers real?

Yes. XRP price, option prices and crash data were pulled from Deribit, Binance, Coinbase and Bitstamp on . Insurance costs are calculated from those real option prices. The example uses XRP at $2.00 for round numbers; costs are a share of the loan, so they hold at any XRP price. The price paths in "Watch it work" are illustrations, not forecasts.
